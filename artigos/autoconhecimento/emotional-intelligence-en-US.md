Emotional Hijacking: When the Brain Loses Control

Joseph LeDoux, a neuroscientist at NYU, explains this phenomenon through two distinct brain pathways:

The Low Road: When a threatening stimulus appears, the amygdala receives information directly from the senses in just 12 milliseconds. It is fast, automatic, and emotional. The response comes before you even realize it.
The High Road: The same information travels to the prefrontal cortex, our executive decision-making center, where it is rationally analyzed. This process takes about 200 milliseconds.
The problem? Under pressure, the Low Road dominates. The amygdala hijacks the prefrontal cortex, and you react impulsively.

## As Daniel Goleman summarizes: "In an emotional emergency, the brain's emotional center proclaims a crisis, recruiting the rest of the brain to its urgent schema."

Emotional intelligence isn't about reacting less; it's about reacting better. It is training the brain to activate the High Road even under stress.

Emotional Intelligence Is Strategy, Not Softness

One of the biggest corporate illusions is believing that emotionally intelligent people never get angry, never feel fear, and never lose control.

That doesn't exist.

True emotional intelligence lies in noticing what is happening inside you and adjusting your response with intention. It is turning emotion into information, not an explosion.

The Concept of Emotional Triggers

Emotional triggers are stimuli that activate automatic responses based on past experiences. They can be words, situations, behaviors, or even tones of voice.

When you understand your own triggers, you begin to see the other person's triggers clearly. This improves your timing, communication, and ability to anticipate reactions.

• Practical Guide: Mapping Your Triggers (Take a Screenshot!)

Step 1 — Identify Recurrent Situations Write down three recent situations that generated a strong emotional reaction:

Meetings where you were interrupted

Step 2 — Analyze the Pattern For each situation, answer:

Which emotion surfaced first?

## Case Study: The Director Who Turned Conflict Into Results

Context: Roberto, Operations Director at a tech multinational with 200 direct reports, faced a chronic issue. In crisis meetings, whenever the team brought up production issues or critical delivery failures, he immediately went into defensive mode.

The Trigger: Any mention of "delay" or "unforeseen problem" activated an immediate reaction of interruption, justification, and shifting blame.

The Discovery: Through executive coaching sessions, Roberto mapped out that this pattern stemmed from childhood experiences, where criticisms of his academic performance were followed by severe punishment. His brain had learned: criticism = threat = immediate defense.

The Solution Applied:

Pre-meeting preparation: 5 minutes of conscious breathing
Personal mantra: "Criticisms are system data, not personal attacks"
Listening protocol: Note 3 points before responding
Explicit validation: Begin responses with "I understand that this situation created..."
Measurable Results in 6 Months:

↓ 65% in interdepartmental conflicts
↑ 40% in decision quality under pressure
↓ Crisis resolution time: from 8 to 4.5 days
↑ Internal leadership NPS: from 62 to 84 (Data collected by HR through internal incident logs, quarterly 360° reviews, and organizational climate surveys)
Emotional Intelligence Applied to Chaos: The Real Test

It is easy to appear emotionally intelligent on vacation, in a quiet home office, or at Sunday lunch.

The real test comes when chaos strikes.

When everything goes wrong at the same time. When someone provokes you. When you are pressured for results.

Applied Technique: The RAIN Protocol Developed by psychologist Tara Brach and validated by mindfulness research at the University of Massachusetts:

R — Recognize: Identify the present emotion without judgment "I am feeling anger right now."
A — Allow: Let the emotion exist without trying to suppress it "It's okay to feel this; it's human."
I — Investigate: Observe where this emotion manifests in your body "I feel tension in my chest and shoulders."
N — Nurture: Treat yourself with compassion "What do I need right now to support myself?"
As Tara Brach states: "The practice of RAIN allows us to recognize the vulnerability of human life and respond with care."

Studies show that this practice significantly reduces amygdala activation and increases activity in the prefrontal cortex.
